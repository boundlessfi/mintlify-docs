> **TL;DR:** A hackathon can offer non-cash prizes, such as a laptop, API credits, or a conference ticket, instead of USDC. You describe each prize in a short line of text. Nothing is held in escrow, and you deliver each prize to its winner yourself.

## What you'll learn

- How to choose non-cash prizes and write your prize list
- How publishing works when there's nothing to fund
- What you can change once the hackathon is live
- How to choose winners and deliver their prizes

---

## Choose the type of rewards

In the **Prizes** step of the hackathon setup, you choose **Cash prize** or **Non-cash prize**. Each hackathon uses one type for all of its prizes.

Cash prizes are paid in USDC and locked in [escrow](/concepts/escrow-and-human-review) when you publish, as described in [Host a hackathon](/organizers/host-a-hackathon). Non-cash prizes are things you hand over yourself, like hardware, AI or cloud credits, event tickets, or travel. Boundless doesn't hold them, so they aren't protected by escrow.

## Write your prize list

Each prize is one short line of text. For example:

- MacBook Pro for the winning team
- $1,000 in OpenAI API credits
- Flight and conference pass for the first-place team

You can add up to 20 prizes, and each line can be up to 200 characters. Drag the lines to change their order. The first line also appears on your hackathon's card when people browse, so put your main prize first.

Keep the lines short and put the full details in the hackathon description, such as who is eligible and how winners receive their prize. The **Prizes** step links to the description so you can jump straight there.

## Plan how you'll deliver each prize

A few kinds of prizes need extra detail in your description:

- AI and cloud credits are usually tied to the winner's own account and expire after a while. Say how the credits will be issued and when they run out.
- For travel, say what happens if a winner can't make the trip, for example if their visa is refused. Consider offering a travel stipend in USDC instead.
- For hardware, say who pays for shipping and any customs fees.

## Non-cash prizes and tracks

Non-cash prizes are overall prizes, so you can't link them to [tracks](/contributors/hackathons/tracks-prizes-judging). If your hackathon already had tracks before you switched to non-cash prizes, entries can't choose them. The **Prizes** step lists those tracks so you can delete them. To keep using tracks, switch back to cash prizes.

## Publish your hackathon

With non-cash prizes there's no funding step.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Submit for review">
    Finish the setup and submit your hackathon. The Boundless team reviews it, just like a cash hackathon.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Publish">
    Once it's approved, click **Publish**. A cash hackathon shows **Fund & publish** here instead. Check the list of prizes in the confirmation, then confirm.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Go live">
    Your hackathon is live and opens to participants on the start date.
  </Step>
</Steps>

You still need to [verify your identity](/start-here/verify-your-identity) before you can publish, the same as for a cash hackathon.

There's no platform fee on a non-cash hackathon, because no money moves through Boundless. See [Fees](/reference/fees).

## Change your prizes after publishing

Once the hackathon is live, you can't switch between cash and non-cash prizes. You can still reword a prize or add new ones, then click **Save prizes**. You can't remove a prize that's already been advertised, or attach a cash amount to it.

When you publish the results, the prizes become final and can't be edited.

## Choose the winners

Judging works the same way as it does for cash prizes. See [How judging works](/organizers/how-judging-works).

Each prize is one winner slot. On the **Winners** page, choose a winner for every prize, or choose not to award it. When every prize is decided, click **Publish results** to confirm the winners.

There's no payout step. Contact each winner and deliver their prize yourself. Boundless doesn't deliver non-cash prizes or track whether they've arrived.

If a team wins a prize that can't be split, such as a single ticket, agree with the team on how it's shared.

## What builders see

The hackathon page lists your prizes in words, each with a gift icon, in place of a prize pool. A note tells builders that you deliver the prizes to the winners. Once results are out, the winners list shows the prize each winner won.

Non-cash wins count as wins on a builder's profile and toward their [reputation](/concepts/reputation). They don't count as earnings, and they don't show up in the builder's wallet.
